# COL1A1 Mutation Analysis in Osteogenesis Imperfecta #
## Disease Background

Osteogenesis imperfecta (OI) is a genetic disorder of connective tissue, commonly known as brittle bone disease, characterized mainly by fragile bones that fracture easily, sometimes with minimal or no trauma. Its major clinical characteristics include recurrent fractures, bone deformities, short stature, blue or gray sclerae, dentinogenesis imperfecta, and hearing loss. The bones and other connective tissues are mainly affected because type I collagen is an important structural component of the bone matrix. OI can result from pathogenic variants in the COL1A1 gene, which encodes the alpha-1 chain of type I collagen. Most COL1A1-related OI is inherited in an autosomal dominant manner, although many cases can also result from new variants. 
## Gene and Normal Protein Function
The official gene symbol is COL1A1, which stands for collagen type I alpha 1 chain. The gene is
located on human chromosome 17, specifically at 17q21.33. It encodes the pro-alpha1(I) chain of type I
collagen, which combines with two alpha chains: two α1 chains and one α2 chain, to form the collagen
triple helix. The normal protein provides structural strength and support to connective tissues, particularly
bone, skin, tendon, and cornea. The protein is synthesized through the endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi
apparatus before being secreted into the extracellular space, where collagen molecules are processed,
assembled into fibrils, and cross-linked to form a strong extracellular matrix. The COL1A1 participates
mainly in collagen biosynthesis, extracellular matrix organization, and connective-tissue structural
support.

## Methods

The normal COL1A1 coding sequence was obtained from the NCBI RefSeq transcript NM_000088.4. The CDS was used as the wild-type reference sequence for manual translation and mutation analysis. The documented mutation, c.1678G>A, was introduced into the wild-type sequence by substituting G with A at nucleotide position 1678. The mutant sequence was then translated and compared with the wild-type protein sequence. Sequence length, reading frame, amino-acid changes, and the presence or absence of a premature stop codon were examined.

An artificial mutation experiment was also performed by deleting one nucleotide from the COL1A1 CDS. The selected artificial mutation was c.981delG, in which one guanine nucleotide was deleted. The resulting sequence was analyzed to determine the expected effects of the deletion on the reading frame and downstream protein sequence.
## Results

The wild-type COL1A1 CDS was 4,395 bp long and produced a protein of 1,464 amino acids. The documented c.1678G>A mutation did not change the CDS length or reading frame. It produced a single amino-acid substitution, p.Gly560Ser, at amino acid position 560. No premature stop codon was observed, and the mutant protein remained 1,464 amino acids long.

The artificial mutation, c.981delG, resulted in the deletion of one nucleotide, reducing the CDS length from 4,395 bp to 4,394 bp. Because one nucleotide was deleted, the reading frame was expected to shift from the deletion site onward. This was predicted to alter many downstream amino acids and potentially produce a premature stop codon and a shortened protein.
## WT versus Mutant Protein Comparison
The WT and mutant proteins first differ at amino-acid position 560, where glycine is replaced by
serine, resulting in the p.Gly560Ser (G560S) substitution. The mutation is a missense mutation because
it changes one amino acid to another without altering the reading frame. Only one amino acid is affected,
and there are no multiple downstream amino-acid changes. No amino acid was inserted or deleted, and
no premature stop codon was produced. The reading frame also remained unchanged because the
mutation was a single-nucleotide substitution. Therefore, the protein length did not change, and both the
WT and mutant proteins remained 1,464 amino acids long.
## Artificial Mutation Experiment
For my artificial mutation, I chose a one-nucleotide deletion in the COL1A1 coding sequence. I
deleted the G at nucleotide position c.981, which is located at the beginning of the 15th sequence line. In
the original sequence, the region is “…CCCCC GTGGC…”, while in the modified sequence it becomes
“…CCCCC TGGC…”, confirming the deletion of one G nucleotide. Because only one nucleotide was
removed, the mutation is predicted to cause a frameshift, changing the grouping of codons from the
deletion site onward. The effect on the protein would probably have the downstream amino-acid sequence
altered and that a premature stop codon may occur, potentially resulting in a shorter and nonfunctional
COL1A1 protein.
## Molecular Interpretation: gene → mutation → protein → cellular effect → phenotype
The COL1A1 gene encodes the alpha-1 chain of type I collagen, which is important for the structural integrity of bone and other connective tissues. In the documented mutation, c.1678G>A changes the codon GGT to AGT, resulting in p.Gly560Ser. This amino-acid substitution occurs in the collagen triple-helical region and may interfere with the normal structure and stability of collagen. Altered collagen structure can affect the organization and mechanical properties of the extracellular matrix in bone, contributing to reduced bone strength and the clinical features of osteogenesis imperfecta, such as bone fragility and recurrent fractures.

The artificial c.981delG mutation differs because it causes a frameshift that can substantially alter the downstream protein sequence and potentially produce a premature stop codon.
## Limitations

The computational analysis can directly identify nucleotide changes, amino-acid substitutions, sequence lengths, reading-frame changes, and predicted protein-sequence effects. However, computational sequence analysis alone cannot fully determine the actual effects of a mutation on protein folding, collagen assembly, cellular processes, or disease severity. The proposed effects of the p.Gly560Ser substitution therefore require support from published experimental and clinical studies. The artificial mutation was also a laboratory-designed mutation and was not necessarily observed in patients with osteogenesis imperfecta.

## Conclusion

The analysis showed that the documented COL1A1 c.1678G>A (p.Gly560Ser) mutation is a missense substitution that changes one amino acid without altering the reading frame or protein length. The mutation may affect the structure and stability of type I collagen because glycine is important in the collagen triple helix. In comparison, the artificial c.981delG mutation removes one nucleotide and is predicted to cause a frameshift, resulting in broader downstream changes to the protein sequence. These results demonstrate how different types and locations of mutations can produce different molecular consequences and potentially contribute to disease.
