# Summary

## WT versus Mutant Protein Comparison

Sequence comparison between the wild-type and documented c.1678G>A mutant COL1A1 protein products reveals that the two sequences first differ at amino acid position 560, where glycine (G) is replaced by serine (S), resulting in p.Gly560Ser (G560S). In computational translation (Frame +1), only this single amino acid is altered, while the downstream amino-acid sequence remains consistent with the wild-type reference sequence. No amino acid insertions, deletions, or premature stop codons were produced. Because the single-base substitution does not shift the triplet reading frame, the computational protein length remains 1,464 amino acids.

The documented mutation is therefore a localized missense change that affects one amino acid without changing the overall length of the COL1A1 protein. However, because glycine is an important residue within the collagen triple-helical structure, the substitution of glycine with serine may affect collagen structure and stability.

## Alignment Results

The sequence comparison between the wild-type and documented c.1678G>A mutant COL1A1 proteins demonstrates a highly conserved protein sequence, with the primary difference occurring at amino acid position 560. The alignment confirms that the single-nucleotide substitution changes the glycine residue to serine without introducing gaps, downstream frameshifts, or additional amino-acid substitutions.

The alignment therefore supports the conclusion that the documented mutation produces a localized amino-acid change rather than a widespread alteration of the protein sequence. Since the reading frame and protein length remain unchanged, the predicted molecular effect is primarily associated with the altered glycine residue and its potential effect on the collagen triple-helical structure.

In comparison, the artificial c.981delG mutation removes one nucleotide and therefore disrupts the normal triplet reading frame. This is expected to produce widespread changes in the downstream amino-acid sequence and may result in a premature stop codon. Thus, the documented substitution and artificial deletion demonstrate how different types of nucleotide changes can produce substantially different effects on protein structure and function.
